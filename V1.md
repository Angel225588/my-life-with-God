## The eleven screens

One story, in the order a hotel lives it:

| # | Screen | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Équipe** | The director sees the departments and who is missing |
| 2 | **Inviter** | Invite by email or phone number, pick the department and role |
| 3 | **Inscription** | The person opens the link: name, photo, short bio, done |
| 4 | **Dépôt du BEO** | Emeline drops the PDF |
| 5 | **Lecture** | Alba shows what it read — services, times, covers — and the contradictions |
| 6 | **Calendrier** | Today at the top: services, first one at 11:15, covers, *2 à trancher* |
| 7 | **Chronologie** | The event hour by hour, with who owns each task |
| 8 | **Tâche** | One task: what, where, for when, the handover button |
| 9 | **Mise en place** | The room set-up task (Hall 78, U for 24, ready by 12:45) |
| 10 | **Mes tâches** | Only mine, in time order |
| 11 | **Profil** | Photo, bio, notifications, sign out |

**Same screens for every role, different content.** A cook and a banquet waiter
see the same calendar — each with their own tasks in it.

## Buttons on screens, voice only in the Alba chat

Every screen works with taps alone. There is no microphone on any screen. Talking
to Alba (typing, a voice message, or a call) happens in one place: the **Alba
chat**, which joins V1. See [`SCREENS.md`](SCREENS.md).

Why: the design leans on Alba understanding a sentence shouted across a kitchen.
If it misreads once in front of a chef, trust is gone and it doesn't come back.
Buttons can't misread.

In the chat, typing works from day one. Voice messages and calls go live
**after** the test below. Alba's spoken voice is ElevenLabs. Every voice action
will still end in *C'est ça / Corriger* — the person confirms before anything is
sent.

## Speech recognition — how we choose

Two steps, two different jobs:

1. **Speech → text.** A speech-recognition model turns the recording into words.
2. **Text → action.** Claude reads the words and works out what to do
   ("3 sont venus mais ils veulent juste un café" → change the task, tell the
   kitchen).

Claude does step 2, not step 1 — it doesn't listen to audio. So step 1 is a
separate choice.

### Candidates

| | Scribe (ElevenLabs) | Whisper (OpenAI) | Voxtral (Mistral) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open weights — can run on our own server | No | Yes | Yes |
| Same provider as Alba's voice | **Yes** — one vendor, one DPA | No | No |
| French company, EU hosting | Check in writing | No | **Yes** — worth something to a French hotel |

All three are good on clean audio. **Neither has been tested on our audio** — a
kitchen at 12:30 with an extractor fan, plates, and three people talking.
That is the only test that counts.

### The test

1. **Record 30 real phrases** during a real service, on a phone held the way staff
   hold it — in the kitchen, the banquet room, and the corridor. Ask first; no
   guest voices; delete the recordings after the test.
2. Write down what was actually said.
3. Run every recording through all three.
4. Score what matters, not overall accuracy:

| Check | Why | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| **Numbers** — "vingt-quatre", "quatorze couverts" | A wrong count is a wrong order | 30/30 |
| **Times** — "treize heures quinze", "midi et demi" | A wrong time is a missed service | 30/30 |
| **Allergens** — "sans gluten", "arachide", "lactose" | A wrong allergen is dangerous | 30/30, no exceptions |
| **Room names** — "Hall 78", "Foyer moins un" | Wrong room, wrong set-up | ≥ 28/30 |
| **Time to text** | Nobody waits holding a tray | < 2 seconds |

Whichever passes wins. If more than one passes, pick on hosting, cost and one-vendor simplicity. If none passes
on allergens, voice never touches allergens — those stay buttons forever.
