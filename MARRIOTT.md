# Marriott — the €149 objection

> ## ⚠️ NO PERMISSION TO USE THE NAME. Confirmed 14 Aug 2026.
>
> Until it exists **in writing**, the name does not appear on the landing page,
> in a video, in a deck, in a DM, on social, or in a cold email. Anywhere public,
> the line is:
>
> > **"An international hotel brand runs their breakfast service on this every day."**
>
> That line does ~90% of the persuasive work with 0% of the risk. Vague is fine —
> prospects fill in the blank generously, and the ones who ask directly can be
> told privately in a real conversation.
>
> Using a global brand's name in marketing without written permission is how a
> solo founder loses the account *and* gets a letter. Not worth it for a
> landing-page logo.
>
> Also: this repo names them throughout. **Keep it private.** If it's ever made
> public, scrub the name first.

**Situation:** They run Alba every day. Quoted €149/month. The director said
*"too expensive for its value."* Payment starts October.

---

## Read the objection correctly

"Too expensive" and "too expensive **for its value**" are different sentences,
and they have opposite solutions.

- *"Too expensive"* = a budget problem → you negotiate.
- *"Too expensive for its value"* = a **measurement** problem → you don't
  negotiate, you count.

He didn't say the price was wrong. He said he can't see what he's getting for it.
Those are solved in opposite directions, and dropping the price solves neither —
it just teaches him the €149 was never real, and every future customer will
eventually learn what Marriott pays.

**Two facts to hold onto before doing anything:**

1. **They use it every day.** Nobody uses a tool daily that has no value. The
   value is real. It has simply never been counted in front of him.
2. **€149/month at a Marriott property is less than one room-night.** Their
   breakfast service earns that back before 8am. The number is not the problem.

Also: €149 is the **Standard tier** in my own price list, and by covers per day
that's probably the correct tier for this property. So my position is honest and
easy to hold: *the price is right, the proof is missing.* I'm not defending a
number I inflated.

---

## What "too expensive for its value" usually actually means

Three possibilities. Find out which one before responding.

**1. Nobody ever measured it.** — Most likely.
He's comparing €149 against a feeling, not a figure. The staff got quieter and
the clipboards disappeared, and nothing visible happened. Invisible value gets
priced at zero. → *Fixable, and it's the whole play below.*

**2. He isn't the one who feels the pain.**
The director doesn't work breakfast service. The F&B manager and the breakfast
supervisor do. The person feeling the relief and the person signing the invoice
are different people, and only one of them has been asked. → *Fixable, and fast.*

**3. It genuinely does less than €149 of work for them.**
Possible. Must be tested honestly. If the measurement in step 2 comes back small,
then the honest answer is a lower tier — not a wounded argument.
→ *I have to be willing to find this out. If I'm not, I'm not measuring, I'm
   campaigning.*

## The play, in order

### Step 1 — Pull the data I already have (this week)

Alba has been running daily in a live hotel for months. That is a dataset most
founders would pay for, and I've never looked at it. From the database:

- Total covers processed, and over how many days
- Days of continuous use without falling back to paper
- **Entitled list vs. actual attendance — the delta.** This is the money.
- Peak throughput: how many guests in the busiest 15 minutes

### Step 2 — Build the one feature worth building right now

> **The monthly report: "What Alba did for you."** One page, automatic.
>
> - `X` covers processed this month
> - `Y` hours of staff time not spent on paper *(covers × seconds saved)*
> - `Z` covers served that weren't on the entitled list — **at €18 each, €N**

This is the exception to *"only build what a paying customer asked for."* It's
not a feature, it's the **argument**. And it keeps working after Marriott:

- It's the answer to this objection, for every customer, forever
- It's the retention mechanism — nobody cancels a tool that emails them a number
  with a euro sign every month
- It's the sales asset for the next 27 properties
- It's a screenshot for the landing page

Build this before the landing page. Build it before the logo.

### Step 3 — Talk to the users before the director

The F&B manager and the breakfast supervisor touch this every morning. Ask them
one question:

> *"If Alba stopped working tomorrow morning, what happens at breakfast?"*

Their answer, in their words, is the value statement — and it's worth more coming
from them than from me. Internal advocacy beats external pitching. A director who
hears "please don't take that away" from his own staff has a different
conversation with me.

### Step 4 — Go back with a report, not a pitch

Not a negotiation. A document.

> *"You said it was too expensive for the value, and that was fair — I hadn't
> shown you the value. So I measured it. Here's your last 30 days."*

Then be quiet and let the page do the work. Then one question:
*"Does that look like €149 to you?"*

If the page is strong, he answers it himself. If the page is weak, I have my
honest answer and I should move to a lower tier without resentment.

## Price options, best to worst

1. **Hold €149. Deliver the report. Revisit in 30 days.**
   Best outcome. Costs nothing but the work I should be doing anyway.
2. **Hold €149, offer annual prepay: €1,490 for the year** (two months free).
   Same monthly price, easier signature, cash up front. Discount the *terms*,
   never the *rate*.
3. **The wager: €99 for three months, then €149 automatically** — unless the
   report shows it isn't worth it, in which case I drop it myself.
   Not a discount. A bet on my own product, which is a strong position to argue
   from and hard to say no to.
4. **Unbundle: €99 core + €50 reporting module.**
   Lets him buy *less* rather than pay less. Protects the rate.

**Never:** quietly drop to €49. It resets my floor permanently, and every future
prospect eventually finds out what the flagship pays.
