### [2026-08-14] Vox — the real thesis: practice with a person you *want* to talk to
Match people not by level, but by "this is someone I'm excited to speak with."
Motivation stops being discipline and becomes anticipation — you progress because
you want the next conversation, not because you scheduled a lesson.

Who pays for it: eventually the learners. Not yet.
Why not now: still parked (see PARKED.md). But **this is the best idea in the
whole Vox file** and it's why Vox will work when it wakes up. Every language app
competes on content. This one competes on *who's on the other side*. Nobody
finishes Duolingo; everybody finishes a conversation with someone they like.

It also reframes the product: Vox is not a teaching tool, it's a **matching**
tool. That's a completely different (and much smaller) thing to build — which
means the first version is a spreadsheet and a Zoom link, not an app.

Guardrail to write down now, before it's ever built: match on **shared purpose
and interest**, never on attraction. The moment "someone you're thrilled to talk
to" becomes "someone you find attractive," this stops being Vox and becomes a
dating app with extra steps — and it inherits every safety and moderation problem
that comes with it. Shared-interest matching, real names, and a hosted room.
